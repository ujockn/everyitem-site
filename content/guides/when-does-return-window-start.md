Order something on a Monday, have it show up on Friday, and you might lose four days of your return window if you count from the wrong day. With windows as short as 14 or 15 days, that's a real risk, and being late can cost money. Amazon, for example, says it may charge a **late fee of 20% of the item price** if you miss your return-by date, and 100% once 30 more days go by, according to the [Amazon Return Policy](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GKM69DUUYKQWKWX7).

The short answer: at most big stores, the clock starts **when the item reaches you**, not when you click "Place order". Walmart is the one with fuzzy wording, and the holiday windows follow their own rules. Below is what each store's official policy says, and where it doesn't say.

**Last checked: Nov 25, 2026.** Every Item is an independent guide and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Best Buy, Amazon, Target, Walmart, Costco or Apple. Stores can change their policies at any time, so confirm on the official page before you rely on a date.

## Best Buy: the day you receive it (the blender question)

If you ordered a blender from Best Buy and want to know whether the clock started at checkout or at your door: it started at your door. Best Buy's [Return & Exchange Policy](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/help-topics/return-exchange-policy/pcmcat260800050014.c?id=pcmcat260800050014) says the return period **begins the day you receive your product**. That applies to new, clearance, open-box, refurbished and pre-owned products. Marketplace items sold by third-party sellers get the same standard window, but not the longer member windows.

**Does the delivery day count as day one?** Best Buy's holiday table says yes: Day 1 is the date you acquire the product. The standard section doesn't spell out the counting, so treat the day it arrives (or the day you pick it up) as day 1. A blender delivered on March 3 would be due back by March 17 on the 15-day window, or May 1 for a My Best Buy Plus or Total member on the 60-day window.

**The holiday window is different.** For 2026, most purchases made **Oct. 30 through Dec. 31** can go back through **Jan. 15, 2027**, no matter when they arrive. Member purchases made Oct. 30 to Dec. 3 get until Jan. 31, 2027. Items bought with a third-party contract (such as phones and other activatable devices, and AppleCare+ plans) and holiday decorations don't get the extension. So a blender ordered on Nov. 25 is fine until Jan. 15, even if it lands in December. Our [Best Buy return policy guide](/guides/best-buy-return-policy/) covers restocking fees and the rest of the rules.

## Amazon: the delivery date, and the return-by date wins

The [Amazon Return Policy](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GKM69DUUYKQWKWX7) gives most items **30 days from delivery**, not from the order date. New Apple and Boost Infinite products get 15 days. Some items get longer, such as most nonperishable baby products and mattresses (90 days).

Amazon doesn't say whether delivery day counts as day one. You don't need to work it out: once you start a return, Amazon shows a **"return by date"** in your confirmation email and in Your Orders, and that's the date it uses. Drop the item off on or before it.

For the 2026 holidays, most items **purchased** Nov. 1 through Dec. 31 can be returned through **Jan. 31, 2027**, and Apple-branded products through Jan. 15, 2027. That window is decided by the purchase date. More detail is in our [Amazon return policy guide](/guides/amazon-return-policy/).

## Target: delivery, Shipt drop-off or store pickup

Target's [return policy article](https://www.target.com/help/article/000061980) says the window for items sold on **Target.com begins once you receive them**: when the order is delivered, delivered by a Shipt shopper, or picked up in store. For Target Plus Partner items (third-party sellers on Target.com), it starts on delivery.

Two things to watch:

- **In-store purchases.** Target's policy doesn't spell out the start for something you bought at the register. The safe move is to count from the date on your receipt.
- **Online orders paid with EBT.** Target's [Returns help page](https://www.target.com/help/articles/returns-exchanges/returns) says these must come back within 90 days of the date the order was **shipped**, delivered by a Shipt shopper, or made ready for pickup. That can be earlier than the day it reaches you.

Target's 2026 holiday rule has its own start date. Per its [Holiday FAQs](https://www.target.com/help/articles/holiday-help/holiday-help-faqs), qualifying purchases made **Nov. 1 through Dec. 24** get a window that **begins Dec. 26, 2026**: electronics and entertainment (not Apple or Beats) and most Target Plus items until Jan. 24, 2027, and Apple, Beats and unlocked or prepaid phones until Jan. 8, 2027. Everything else stays on the normal policy. See our [Target return policy guide](/guides/target-return-policy/) for the full list of windows.

## Walmart: "after purchase or upon receipt"

[Walmart's Return Policy](https://corporate.walmart.com/policies) (updated Nov. 2, 2026) gives most items **90 days after purchase or upon receipt**. It doesn't say which date applies to a Walmart.com order that ships a few days later. For a store purchase, the two are the same day. For an online order, count from the order date to be safe, and treat any extra days as a bonus.

A few Walmart items do name a delivery-based start:

- **Major appliances** (large refrigerators, ranges, washers and the like): full refund within **2 days of accepted delivery**.
- **Small appliances, dorm-size and wine refrigerators sold by Marketplace sellers**: **30 days of delivery**.

Walmart's 2026 holiday window is decided by purchase date: most items **bought Oct. 1 through Dec. 31** can be returned until **Jan. 31**, with exceptions such as wireless phones, protection plans, major appliances and Dyson products. Our [Walmart return policy guide](/guides/walmart-return-policy/) has the full window table.

## Costco: when you received it, for the items that have a deadline

[Costco's return policy](https://customerservice.costco.com/app/answers/answer_view/a_id/1191) lists a **90-day limit, counted from the date you received the item**, for TVs, projectors, major appliances, computers, touchscreen tablets, smart wearables, cameras, drones, camcorders, MP3 players and cell phones (cell phone details vary by carrier contract). Recreational carts ordered online follow the same 90 days from receipt.

For most other products, Costco's satisfaction guarantee doesn't list a deadline, though it notes that limitations apply. Costco doesn't post a holiday extension. Our [Costco return policy guide](/guides/costco-return-policy/) lists the exceptions.

## Apple: 14 days from the day you receive it

Apple's [U.S. Sales and Refund Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/sales-support/sales-policies/retail_us.html) gives you **14 days from the date you receive the product** from Apple, with a receipt. That covers Apple Store locations, Apple Store Online, the Apple Store app and phone orders. Apple doesn't describe pickup orders separately. The natural reading is that you receive the item when you pick it up, so keep the pickup confirmation as your proof of the date.

Apple's holiday rule is the one that goes by **delivery**, not purchase. Its [Returns & Refunds page](https://www.apple.com/shop/help/returns_refund) says eligible Apple Store Online products **received** Nov. 6 through Dec. 25, 2026 can come back through **Jan. 8, 2027**. Carrier-financed iPhones (T-Mobile, Verizon) and Verizon-financed iPads aren't included. A gift ordered Dec. 20 that arrives Dec. 27 misses it and gets the normal 14 days, so Jan. 10, 2027. More in our [Apple return policy guide](/guides/apple-return-policy/).

## Holiday windows flip the rule

During the holidays, most stores swap "when you got it" for "when you bought it":

| Store | 2026 holiday window is based on | Dates | Last day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Best Buy | Purchase date | Bought Oct. 30 to Dec. 31 | Jan. 15, 2027 (members who bought by Dec. 3: Jan. 31) |
| Amazon | Purchase date | Bought Nov. 1 to Dec. 31 | Jan. 31, 2027 (Apple-branded: Jan. 15) |
| Walmart | Purchase date | Bought Oct. 1 to Dec. 31 | Jan. 31 |
| Target | Purchase date, with the clock starting Dec. 26 | Bought Nov. 1 to Dec. 24 (listed categories only) | Jan. 24 or Jan. 8, 2027 |
| Apple | Date received | Received Nov. 6 to Dec. 25 | Jan. 8, 2027 |
| Costco | No holiday window posted | - | Normal rules |

Each store excludes some items, mainly phones, appliances and protection plans. Our [holiday return deadlines guide](/guides/holiday-return-deadlines-2026/) walks through each store's exclusions with worked examples.

## How to find your real last day

1. **Find the right start date.** For delivered orders, that's the delivery date in your order history or carrier tracking, not the order confirmation. For pickup, it's the pickup day. For Walmart online orders, use the order date to be safe.
2. **Check whether a holiday window applies.** If it does, use its fixed last day instead of counting.
3. **Count with the start day as day 1** unless the store shows you a return-by date. If it does, as Amazon does, trust that date.
4. **Write the deadline down the day the item arrives**, and set a reminder a few days early.
5. **If you've already missed it,** read [what you can still do after a missed return window](/guides/missed-return-window/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Best Buy's return period start on the order date or the day I receive it?

The day you receive it. Best Buy's policy says the return period begins the day you receive your product, and its holiday table counts that day as Day 1. For 2026 holiday purchases (Oct. 30 to Dec. 31), most items can go back through Jan. 15, 2027 regardless of delivery date.

### Does Amazon's 30-day return window start when I order?

No. Amazon counts 30 days from delivery for most items. After you start a return, use the return-by date Amazon shows in Your Orders and your confirmation email.

### Does picking up an order in store start the return clock?

At Target and Best Buy, yes: Target says the Target.com window starts when you pick up the order, and Best Buy counts from the day you get the product. Apple counts from the date you receive the item and doesn't treat pickup separately, so count from the day you collect it.

### Does the delivery day count as day one?

Best Buy says it does in its holiday table. Amazon, Target, Walmart, Costco and Apple don't spell it out. Count the arrival day as day 1 so you never run a day late, and rely on any return-by date the store shows you.

### When does the return window start for a gift?

The store's clock runs from the original purchase or delivery, not from the day the gift is opened. Ask the giver for the order and delivery dates, then check whether a holiday window applies.

### Does a late delivery give me more time during the holidays?

Only at Apple, whose 2026 holiday window is based on the date you received the item (Nov. 6 to Dec. 25). Best Buy, Amazon, Walmart and Target base their holiday windows on the purchase date, so delivery timing doesn't change the last day for items that qualify.
