This guide explains Amazon's return damage fee in plain English: how much it is, what triggers it, how it compares with Amazon's other return fees, and what you can do to avoid it. Every detail comes from Amazon's official help pages, linked in [Sources](#sources). Policies change, so check the official pages before you rely on them.

**Last checked: Oct 29, 2026.** Every Item is an independent guide and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Amazon.

## How much is the Amazon damage fee?

According to the [Amazon Return Policy](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GKM69DUUYKQWKWX7), the damage fee is **up to 50% of the item price**. Items from **Amazon Luxury** are charged **100% of the item price**.

The fee comes out of your refund. Amazon's [Refunds page](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GKQNFKFK5CF3C54B) calls this a **partial refund**: "All returns are inspected against the expected item," and refunds are reduced for signs of customer use, damage, or missing parts, accessories or manuals.

On a $200 item, for example, a damage fee of up to 50% could mean a refund as low as $100. Amazon doesn't publish a fixed amount below that cap, so the fee can be less than 50%.

**You can't get the item back.** Amazon says an item can't be sent back to you after it has been returned, except certain Amazon Luxury items that incur the 100% damage fee.

## What triggers a damage fee?

Amazon says you may be charged a damage fee when you return an item that:

- is **damaged**
- is **missing parts**
- is **not in original condition**
- has **tags removed**
- has **obvious signs of use**

The fee applies only when the problem is **not due to an Amazon or seller error**. If the item arrived damaged, defective or wrong, that's a different situation (see below).

Amazon's return policy also spells out what "original condition" means: return the item unused, with tags attached and hygiene seals and liners intact, in the original manufacturer's packaging, including tags, components, accessories, manuals, certificates of authenticity and other inserts.

## Damage fee vs. late fee vs. restocking fee

Amazon lists four kinds of return fees. Only one is about the item's condition:

| Fee | When it applies | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Damage fee | Item is damaged, missing parts, not in original condition, has tags removed or shows obvious use (not Amazon's or the seller's fault) | Up to 50% of the item price; 100% for Amazon Luxury |
| Late fee | You don't drop off the item or complete a carrier pickup on or before the return by date | 20% of the item price for the first 30 days after the return by date; 100% after that |
| Restocking fee | Opened, activated, used or incomplete software and video games; opened collectible cards, board games and collectible figurines | 100% of the item price |
| Return shipping fee | You choose a drop-off or shipping option that isn't free, return a heavy or bulky item, or have an unusually high return rate | Varies by item and shipping method |

The key differences:

- **The damage fee depends on condition.** It's a range ("up to 50%"), not a fixed amount.
- **The late fee depends on timing.** An item in perfect condition can still lose 20% of its value if you miss the return by date.
- **The restocking fee depends on the product type.** For the listed categories it's 100%, so an opened video game or board game effectively gets no refund.

Amazon's Refunds page lists the restocking fee only for opened collectible cards, board games and figurines, while the Return Policy page also includes software and video games. Check the product page and your return confirmation for the item you're sending back.

## Third-party sellers

When a third-party seller ships its own inventory, your return goes back to the seller, not Amazon. According to [Returns to Third-Party Sellers](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=G38BHJQ25PNCLUBU), the seller must give you a US return address, a prepaid return label, or a full refund without asking for the item back. The seller usually processes your refund within two business days of receiving the return.

Amazon says refunds from sellers can be delayed if an item that was sold as new comes back **with signs of use or damage**. Under the [A-to-z Guarantee](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GQ37ZCNECJKTFYQV), a seller may deduct **restocking fees** from returns made for "any other reason" (not because the item was damaged, defective or materially different from what you ordered).

To protect yourself when returning to a seller, Amazon recommends:

- Use the original packaging or a similar-sized box.
- Use the seller's shipping label.
- For items over $35, use a trackable shipping service. For items worth $100 or more, insure the shipment and use a signature service.

Amazon says it may not be able to cover you under the A-to-z Guarantee if the package doesn't reach the seller and you didn't use a trackable shipping method.

## What to do if you think the fee is unfair

**For items sold or shipped by Amazon:** contact Amazon Customer Service. We didn't find a separate dispute process for damage fees on Amazon's help pages; they point you to Customer Service for refund problems, and the Refunds page says to contact Customer Service if you returned an item more than 30 days ago and haven't received a refund notification. Have your order number, the return confirmation, and your photos of the item ready.

**For items from a third-party seller:** contact the seller first. If the seller doesn't respond within 48 hours, Amazon says you can request an A-to-z Guarantee refund if you returned the item in line with the Amazon Return Policy but didn't receive the expected refund. Amazon tells you to check the Return Fees section of its return policy before you file. To request one, go to Your Orders, find the order, choose **Problem with order**, select your problem and choose **Request refund**, as described on [Request an A-to-z Guarantee Refund](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GSZAYH7K2C2NVNC9). Amazon says reviews can take up to one week.

Two A-to-z rules to know:

- Your order's last possible delivery date must be **90 days ago or less**.
- If you ask your bank or card issuer for a refund or chargeback, you're **no longer eligible** for an A-to-z Guarantee refund.

**If the item arrived damaged or defective:** the damage fee is for problems that aren't Amazon's or the seller's fault. Choose the return reason that matches what actually happened, and take photos of the damage when it arrives.

## How to avoid an Amazon damage fee

- **Take photos before you pack.** Photograph the item from every side, the accessories laid out, the tags, and the packing slip. It's your best evidence if your refund comes back reduced.
- **Keep the box and every part** until you're sure you're keeping the item: manuals, cables, inserts, tags and certificates of authenticity.
- **Leave tags on and seals intact** while you decide. Amazon lists removed tags and broken hygiene seals as condition problems.
- **Erase your personal data** from computers, phones and other devices before sending them back, following the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Return the whole bundle.** Amazon says you must return every item in a "Bundle with Savings" to get a refund.
- **Drop it off before the return by date.** Being late can trigger a separate late fee of 20% of the item price, even if the item is in perfect condition.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the damage fee on Amazon?

Up to 50% of the item price. Amazon Luxury items are charged 100% of the item price. The fee is taken out of your refund.

### What counts as damage for an Amazon return?

Amazon may charge the fee if the item is damaged, missing parts, not in original condition, has tags removed or shows obvious signs of use, for reasons that aren't Amazon's or the seller's fault.

### Does Amazon charge a damage fee if the item arrived damaged?

The damage fee applies only when the problem is not due to an Amazon or seller error. If the item arrived damaged or defective, say so in your return reason and keep photos of the damage.

### Is the damage fee the same as a restocking fee?

No. The damage fee depends on the item's condition and is up to 50%. The restocking fee is 100% of the item price for specific product types, such as opened collectible cards, board games and figurines.

### Can I get my item back if Amazon charges a damage fee?

Generally no. Amazon says an item can't be sent back to you after it has been returned, except certain Amazon Luxury items that incur a 100% damage fee.

### How do I dispute an Amazon damage fee?

For items sold by Amazon, contact Amazon Customer Service. For third-party sellers, contact the seller first, then request an A-to-z Guarantee refund if they don't respond within 48 hours and you returned the item in line with Amazon's policy.
