Return an opened digital camera to Best Buy and you can lose **15% of the price** to a restocking fee, per [Best Buy's Return & Exchange Policy](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/help-topics/return-exchange-policy/pcmcat260800050014.c?id=pcmcat260800050014), and send back an opened video game to Amazon and it can keep **100%**, per the [Amazon Return Policy](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GKM69DUUYKQWKWX7). Most other opened gadgets are less dramatic, but each store has its own rules for earbuds, cameras, calculators, computer parts and games. This guide takes those five items store by store: Amazon, Target, Walmart, Best Buy and Costco. Every detail comes from official store pages, linked in [Sources](#sources). Policies change, so check the official page before you rely on it.

**Last checked: Nov 5, 2026.** Every Item is an independent guide and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Amazon, Target, Walmart, Best Buy or Costco.

## Opened earbuds and headphones

Earbuds are the item people ask about most, because you can't judge them until they're in your ears.

- **Target:** earbuds aren't on [Target's final-sale list](https://www.target.com/help/articles/returns-exchanges/returns). Apple and Beats products must come back within **14 days**, and other electronics and entertainment items within **30 days**. Target also says items that are opened or damaged, or that come without a receipt, may be denied a refund or exchange, so it's not a promise. Target Owned Brand items get **one year with a receipt**, and Target's page lists Electronics among the categories that guarantee covers. The page doesn't say which earbuds are Owned Brand, so check the product page.
- **Best Buy:** most products have **15 days** from the day you receive them (60 days for paid My Best Buy Plus and Total members). Items must be in like-new condition and returned with all accessories and packaging, or Best Buy may deny the return or deduct for what's missing. Best Buy also lists "products that interact with bodily fluids" as nonreturnable once opened and doesn't say whether earbuds count, so ask before you open a pair you're unsure about.
- **Amazon:** items must go back in original or unused condition with hygiene seals and liners intact and in the original packaging with every accessory. Missing parts or obvious signs of use can trigger a damage fee of up to 50%. Check the product page, since Amazon says each item's return eligibility is shown there.
- **Walmart:** the [policy](https://corporate.walmart.com/policies) gives **consumer electronics 30 days** and doesn't list a rule specific to earbuds. It doesn't define "consumer electronics" on that page, so rely on the return-by date in your purchase history.
- **Costco:** headphones aren't on the [90-day electronics list](https://customerservice.costco.com/app/answers/answer_view/a_id/1191), and the policy lists no general deadline for other products. Costco adds that limitations apply.

## Opened cameras and point-and-shoots

This is where opening the box costs real money.

- **Best Buy** charges a restocking fee of **15% of the purchase price** on digital cameras, camera lenses and camcorders (including action cameras), along with drones and projectors, among others. Best Buy says there's no fee if the product is **unopened**, or if you bought it in Alabama, Colorado, Hawaii, Iowa, Mississippi, Ohio, Oklahoma or South Carolina, or where the law prohibits it. A point-and-shoot is a digital camera, so it's on the list. The fee still applies in the holiday period. Best Buy's page says that if a product is damaged in shipping or defective, you should return it within the window and it will arrange a replacement, but it doesn't say how the fee works in that case, so ask the store.
- **Costco:** cameras are on its 90-day electronics list, counted from the day you received the item. The policy doesn't mention a restocking fee.
- **Amazon:** no camera-specific fee. The general rules apply: 30 days, original condition, and a damage fee of up to 50% for missing parts or obvious use.
- **Target and Walmart:** both give electronics 30 days and list no camera fee. Target's only listed restocking charge is up to $35 on opened phones.

Before you hand any camera back, remove your memory card and photos. Amazon and Walmart both tell you to erase personal information from devices, and Best Buy says connected and Wi-Fi devices must be wiped and unlinked from any cloud account first.

## Opened calculators

No store lists calculators as an exception, so the question is the window and the condition of the item.

- **Target:** calculators aren't final sale or exchange-only. Target's electronics window is 30 days, but it doesn't say whether calculators count as electronics. If they don't, the standard 90 days for most items sold by Target would apply. Your receipt or the product page shows the return-by date for your item.
- **Walmart:** the same uncertainty applies. Consumer electronics get 30 days, most other items 90, and the page doesn't classify calculators.
- **Best Buy:** 15 days, like-new condition, with the cover, cables and packaging.
- **Amazon:** 30 days from delivery, in original or unused condition.
- **Costco:** no deadline is listed for calculators.

If you bought a calculator for a class, return it before the term gets busy. The shortest window you'll face is Best Buy's 15 days.

## Opened motherboards and computer parts

Computer parts are the one category where only one store spells out the rule.

- **Walmart:** the policy lists "PCs and PC components", with motherboards, RAM and graphics cards as examples, at **30 days**. They must go back in **original packaging**, and after 30 days Walmart sends you to the manufacturer for warranty help. Items with a rechargeable battery must include the original battery.
- **Amazon:** the policy has no PC-parts section. Items sold on Amazon.com get 30 days from delivery, and you must send items back in original or unused condition with all components and manuals. A damage fee of up to 50% applies to items with obvious signs of use. The 100% restocking fee is only for software and video games, collectible cards, board games and collectible figurines, not for hardware. If a third-party seller shipped the part, [the return goes to the seller](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=G38BHJQ25PNCLUBU), who must offer a US return address, a prepaid label or a full refund without a return. Amazon says refunds can be delayed if an item sold as new comes back with signs of use or damage.
- **Best Buy:** no rule specific to PC parts. The general rules apply: 15 days, like-new condition, all contents and packaging.
- **Costco:** the policy gives computers 90 days from when you received them. It doesn't say whether loose parts count.
- **Target:** no rule for PC parts is listed.

**Practical tip:** a motherboard is easiest to return before you install it. Keep the box, the anti-static bag, the socket cover if there is one, and the accessories. Stores are checking for exactly these.

## Opened video games

Games are the item with the strictest written rules.

| Store | What the policy says about opened physical games |
| --- | --- |
| Amazon | Software and video games that are opened, activated, used or missing parts carry a **restocking fee of 100% of the item price** |
| Target | Opened music, movies, video games and software can only be **exchanged for the same title**, on the same or a different gaming platform |
| Walmart | Opened physical games aren't singled out on the policy page. **Downloadable** video games and software can't be returned, and electronics bundles must be returned together |
| Best Buy | Opened physical software, movies, music, video games and books bought from Best Buy can only be **exchanged for the identical item**. Digital games can't be returned |
| Costco | Games aren't listed as an exception. No deadline is listed |

Target's page doesn't say whether games fall under its 30-day electronics-and-entertainment window, so use the return-by date on your receipt.

## What about the holidays?

Holiday gifts get extra time at some stores but not all. Amazon says most items bought between Nov. 1 and Dec. 31, 2026 can go back through **Jan. 31, 2027**. Best Buy says most purchases made Oct. 30 through Dec. 31 can go back through **Jan. 15, 2027**. Target says electronics and entertainment bought Nov. 1 through Dec. 24 can go back through **Jan. 24, 2027** (Apple and Beats: Jan. 8). Walmart lists most items bought Oct. 1 through Dec. 31 as returnable until **Jan. 31**, with exceptions. Costco's return policy doesn't mention a holiday window. Restocking fees and exchange-only rules still apply, so a holiday extension doesn't make an opened camera free to return. ## How to return an opened electronic item without losing money

1. **Find the item's real deadline.** Look at the receipt, your order page or the product page, not the store's headline number. Electronics often have a shorter window.
2. **Read the fee before you open it.** For cameras, games and phones, the cost of opening the box is written in the policy.
3. **Keep every part.** Box, inserts, cables, ear tips, cases, manuals and protective covers. Amazon, Best Buy and Walmart all tie refunds to original packaging or complete contents.
4. **Wipe your data.** Remove photos, accounts and pairings from cameras, headphones with apps and other connected devices before you return them.
5. **Take photos of the item and its packaging** before you drop it off, in case there's a dispute about condition.
6. **Return in person if the item is high value.** Target says that on mail returns it may charge you again if the item fails inspection after a refund was issued.

## How to never miss the return window on electronics

- **Photograph the receipt the day you buy** and note the return-by date.
- **Test the item in the first few days.** Most electronics windows are 14 to 30 days, and Best Buy's is only 15.
- **Keep the box until the window closes.** Missing packaging is the easiest reason for a store to deny a return.
- **Set a reminder a few days early.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you return opened earbuds?

Often, if you're inside the window and everything is in the box. Target doesn't list earbuds as final sale (Apple and Beats get 14 days, other electronics 30), but it says opened items may be denied. Best Buy won't take back opened products that interact with bodily fluids and doesn't say whether earbuds count, so ask first.

### Does Best Buy charge a restocking fee on a point-and-shoot camera?

Yes. Best Buy charges 15% of the purchase price on digital cameras, lenses and camcorders. There's no fee if the camera is unopened, or if you bought it in AL, CO, HI, IA, MS, OH, OK or SC, or where the law prohibits it.

### Can you return an opened calculator to Target?

Target doesn't list calculators as final sale or exchange-only, but it also says opened items may be denied. Check your receipt for the return-by date, since electronics have 30 days.

### Can you return a motherboard to Amazon?

Amazon's policy has no separate rule for computer parts. Most items can go back within 30 days of delivery in original or unused condition, and a damage fee of up to 50% can apply to items with obvious use. Parts sold by third-party sellers go back to the seller.

### Can you return opened video games to Target?

Target only exchanges opened video games, for the same title on the same or a different platform. It doesn't refund them.

### Which store gives the longest return time on electronics?

Costco gives 90 days on cameras, computers, TVs, tablets and phones, counted from when you received the item. Best Buy gives most products only 15 days, or 60 for paid My Best Buy Plus and Total members.
