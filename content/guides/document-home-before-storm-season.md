Seasonal weather is easier to plan for when the paperwork is already done. Ready.gov and FEMA ask households to document belongings and keep important records somewhere they can still reach after a disruption. This short guide is a calm prep checklist for that step—not a forecast, not a countdown.

**Last checked: Nov 4, 2026.** Every Item is an independent guide and is not affiliated with FEMA, Ready.gov or any insurer. Your policy decides what is covered; ask your agent what proof they want.

For the full room-by-room list of what to record, see [how to make a home inventory for insurance](/guides/home-inventory-for-insurance/).

## Why document before the season

FEMA's Ready.gov financial preparedness guidance says to gather household records and [document and insure your property](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2020-03/ready_document-and-insure-your-property.pdf) ahead of time. A written inventory, with photos or video, helps you show what you owned and can speed a claim. Doing that work on an ordinary weekend is simpler than trying to rebuild a list from memory later.

This is ordinary record-keeping. It does not prevent damage, and it is not a substitute for the right insurance for your area.

## A calm weekend checklist

1. **Pick one or two rooms to start.** Electronics, jewelry, appliances and furniture first; smaller items still matter, but the high-value list is enough for a first pass.
2. **Photograph or slowly video each room.** Open closets and cabinets. Narrate brands and models if that is easier than typing.
3. **Write down details for items that matter.** Name, brand, model, serial number, roughly when you bought it and what it cost. Attach receipts or order confirmations where you have them.
4. **Note the room.** Grouping by room makes gaps obvious and helps an adjuster follow the list.
5. **Export a copy you can open elsewhere.** A PDF or similar file you can email to yourself, keep in a cloud drive or share with someone who lives somewhere else.

Most people can cover a room in about 10–15 minutes once they have a rhythm. You do not have to finish the whole house in one day.

## Keep the copy somewhere you can still reach

An inventory that only lives on a phone that stays in the house is easy to lose with everything else. Ready.gov suggests storing important documents in a safe deposit box, on an external drive or in the cloud so you can open them after a disruption.

Practical options:

- A cloud drive you can sign into from another device
- An email to yourself with the PDF attached
- A trusted person who lives somewhere else
- A USB drive kept off-site or in a fire-resistant safe

Update those copies when you add something valuable, or when you review the list each year (policy renewal is a natural reminder).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a separate inventory for every type of weather risk?

No. One clear household inventory works for insurance documentation whether the risk in your area is storms, wildfire, flood or something else. Review your policy for what hazards it covers; flood coverage, for example, is often separate.

### Is a video walkthrough enough on its own?

It is a strong start and better than nothing. Adding brands, models, serial numbers and values makes a claim easier to process. See the [full home inventory checklist](/guides/home-inventory-for-insurance/) for what to record per item.

### When should I update the list?

Add major purchases when you buy them, and review the whole list about once a year—before your local storm or wildfire season, or when your policy renews.

### Does documenting belongings replace insurance?

No. Documentation supports a claim; it does not create coverage. Talk with your agent about whether your limits and hazard coverage match your home and belongings.
