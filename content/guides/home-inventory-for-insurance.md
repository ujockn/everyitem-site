A home inventory is a list of what you own, with photos and proof of value. If there's a fire, flood or burglary, it can make an insurance claim much faster and help you avoid forgetting things when you're under stress. This guide shows you how to make one in an afternoon.

**Last checked: Nov 1, 2026.** Every Item is an independent guide and is not affiliated with any insurer. Your policy's terms decide what's covered, so check them, or ask your agent what documentation they want.

## What to record for each item

| Field | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Photo (and a photo of the label) | Shows you owned it and what condition it was in |
| Name, brand and model | Makes it possible to price an exact replacement |
| Serial number | Helps for stolen items and police reports |
| Purchase date and price | Supports the value you claim |
| Receipt or order confirmation | The strongest proof of purchase |
| Room | Speeds up the claim and helps you spot gaps |

## Room-by-room checklist

- **Living room:** TV, speakers, game consoles, furniture, rugs, lamps, art, books
- **Kitchen:** appliances (fridge, range, dishwasher, microwave), small appliances, cookware, dishes
- **Bedrooms:** mattresses and frames, dressers, clothing, shoes, jewelry, watches
- **Home office:** computers, monitors, printers, desk and chair, cameras
- **Bathrooms:** hair tools, linens, cabinet contents
- **Garage and shed:** tools, bikes, lawn equipment, sports gear, freezers
- **Closets and storage:** luggage, seasonal decor, collectibles

Don't skip the small things. Clothing, kitchenware and books add up fast when you have to replace all of them at once.

## How to do it in one afternoon

1. **Start with the expensive stuff.** Electronics, jewelry, appliances and furniture first.
2. **Take a slow video walkthrough** of each room. Open closets and drawers and narrate what you see.
3. **Add photos and details** to the items worth more than about $100.
4. **Attach receipts** where you have them. Order histories at Amazon, Walmart and Target can show older purchases.
5. **Total each room.** This helps you check whether your coverage limit is high enough.

## Keep a copy somewhere safe

An inventory that burns with the house doesn't help. Export it as a PDF and keep digital copies in at least two places outside your home, so losing your phone or your house doesn't mean losing the record:

- **A cloud drive** such as iCloud Drive, Google Drive or Dropbox, so you can open it from any device after a disaster.
- **Your email.** Send the PDF to yourself so it's searchable and easy to forward to your insurer.
- **A trusted person.** Share a copy with a family member or friend who lives somewhere else.
- **A USB drive or external disk** kept in a fire-resistant safe, a safe deposit box or at someone else's home.

Update every copy whenever you add something valuable. Old copies may miss your newest purchases.

## Keep it up to date

Add new purchases when you buy them, and review the whole list once a year, for example when your policy renews.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need receipts for a home insurance claim?

Not always, but proof helps. Photos, videos, serial numbers, bank or card statements and order histories can all support a claim. Ask your insurer what they accept.

### Is a video walkthrough enough?

It's a strong start and much better than nothing. A list with brands, models and values makes the claim faster.

### How often should I update my home inventory?

Add big purchases right away, and review the whole list once a year.

### Where should I store my home inventory?

Keep digital copies away from your home, such as in a cloud drive, in your email and with a trusted person, so a fire, flood or theft can't destroy both your belongings and the record of them.
