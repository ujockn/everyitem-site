Miss Amazon's return-by date and you can lose **20% of the item price** to a late fee, or **all of it** once 30 more days go by, according to the [Amazon Return Policy](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GKM69DUUYKQWKWX7). The good news: a closed window isn't always the end. This guide covers what you can still try, starting with Amazon and then Target, Walmart, Best Buy and Costco. Every detail comes from official store and card issuer pages, linked in [Sources](#sources).

**Last checked: Oct 30, 2026.** Every Item is an independent guide and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Amazon, Target, Walmart, Best Buy, Costco, American Express or Chase.

## First: which situation are you in?

Your options depend on two questions: did you start the return before the deadline, and is something actually wrong with the item?

| Your situation | Best next step |
| --- | --- |
| You started an Amazon return but didn't drop it off in time | Drop it off now. Amazon's late fee is 20% for the first 30 days after the return-by date |
| You never started the return, and the "Return Items" button is gone | Contact Amazon Customer Service and ask for an exception (not guaranteed) |
| The item is broken, defective or not as described | Use Amazon's Product Support or "Problem with order" options, or the manufacturer's warranty |
| You bought from a third-party seller on Amazon | Contact the seller; the A-to-z Guarantee may help within 90 days if the item is faulty or the seller won't cooperate |
| You just changed your mind, and the store says no | Check whether your credit card has return protection |

## Amazon: you started the return but missed the drop-off

This is the "missed it by one day" case with the best odds. Amazon's policy gives you a "return by date" in your return confirmation email and in the Return Request tab of Your Orders. If you don't drop off the item or complete a carrier pickup **on or before** that date, Amazon says it may charge a late fee:

| When the item is dropped off | Late fee |
| --- | --- |
| On or before the return-by date | None |
| Up to 30 days after the return-by date | 20% of the item price |
| More than 30 days after the return-by date | 100% of the item price |

So if you're a day late, don't give up on the return. Drop it off as soon as you can and you may still get most of your money back. The fee is a maximum ("may be charged"), but plan on losing 20%. Late fees stack with any other return fees, such as a damage fee if the item isn't in original condition. See our [Amazon return damage fee guide](/guides/amazon-return-damage-fee/) for how that works.

## Amazon: you never started the return

If the return window has closed and you never started a return, the "Return Items" option usually disappears from Your Orders. Amazon's return policy doesn't promise late returns for change-of-mind purchases. Your only route is to ask:

1. Sign in and go to Amazon's [Customer Service page](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html), then choose the option to contact an associate.
2. Have the order number ready, plus the delivery date and the reason you're late.
3. Ask whether they can open a return for the item. Be polite and be specific about what happened.

Amazon decides these case by case, and the answer may be no. If an associate does agree, ask them to confirm the new return-by date in writing (in the chat or by email) before you send the item, and drop it off right away.

## Amazon: the item is defective or broken

A faulty item is a different problem from a missed deadline, and you have more options:

- **Amazon Product Support.** Amazon's [Get Product Support](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=G94W2A3Y2Y6EQQPG) page says it offers free help with setup, troubleshooting, replacement parts and **warranty repairs**, by phone, chat or guides. Availability depends on the item. If support isn't available, Amazon points you to Your Orders for damaged, defective or broken items.
- **Your Orders.** Amazon's [Replace a Damaged, Defective, or Broken Item](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html/?nodeId=GP7Z9RS868ZP5J9F) page says Your Orders will show what options you have.
- **The manufacturer's warranty.** Amazon's return policy tells you to contact the manufacturer directly for warranty questions, and notes that a manufacturer warranty may not cover used products. Check the warranty details on the product page or in the box. You'll usually need proof of purchase, so download the invoice from Your Orders.

## Amazon: third-party sellers and the A-to-z Guarantee

If a third-party seller shipped the item, the return goes to the seller, so contact them first from Your Orders. If that doesn't work, Amazon's [A-to-z Guarantee](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GQ37ZCNECJKTFYQV) may help, but only in specific cases. You can request a refund if:

- you ordered from a third-party seller, **and**
- the order's last possible delivery date was **90 days ago or less**, **and**
- one of Amazon's listed problems applies. For returns, that includes an item that's damaged, defective or not as described where the seller hasn't authorized your return within 48 hours, a return request the seller closed or didn't authorize, or a return you sent in line with Amazon's policies without getting the refund you expected.

A-to-z is **not** a way to return a working item late just because you changed your mind. Amazon's refund amounts assume you requested the return within 30 days of delivery and sent it within 14 days of starting it. Two more rules matter:

- If you ask your bank or card issuer for a refund or chargeback, you're **no longer eligible** for an A-to-z refund.
- A-to-z doesn't cover digital items, services, stored value instruments, Spot Buys or automobiles.

To file, go to Your Orders, choose **Problem with order**, and follow the steps on [Request an A-to-z Guarantee Refund](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GSZAYH7K2C2NVNC9).

## Credit card return protection

A few credit cards will refund an eligible item when the store refuses a return. It's not a standard benefit: the programs below cover only specific cards. Check your card's guide to benefits or call the number on the back before you count on it.

| Card benefit | Time limit | Limits | Key conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [American Express Return Protection](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/credit-cards/features-benefits/policies/return-protection-terms.html) (listed cards only, such as the Platinum Card and Blue Cash Preferred) | 90 days from purchase | Up to $300 per item and $1,000 per card account per calendar year | Charged in full to the eligible card; the merchant must have refused the return; item in original condition and working |
| [Chase Return Protection](https://www.chase.com/personal/credit-cards/education/basics/how-chase-return-protection-works) (Chase Sapphire Reserve) | 90 days from purchase | Up to $500 per item and $1,000 per account in 12 months | Charged in full to the card; the retailer must refuse the return; notify the benefit administrator within 90 days |

Both programs exclude many categories. Amex's [benefit terms](https://www.americanexpress.com/content/dam/amex/us/credit-cards/features-benefits/RP_Benefit_Guide_Rev_10-20_10.09.23-final.pdf) exclude items such as jewelry, watches, computer software, perishables and used items. Chase's list includes damaged or non-working items, computer software and seasonal items. You'll typically need the **original receipt** and your **card statement**, and you may have to send the item in.

The 90 days count from the **purchase date**, not delivery, so a 30-day Amazon return window that just closed usually leaves you time to file.

## How to never miss a return window again

- **Write down the real deadline the day the item arrives.** Amazon and Best Buy count from delivery or receipt, not from the order date.
- **Start the return early if you're unsure.** On Amazon, a late drop-off costs 20%; a return you never started may cost you everything.
- **Know your store's shortest windows.** Amazon gives new Apple products 15 days, Best Buy gives most items 15 days, and Target gives Apple products 14.
- **Keep the box, tags and receipt** until you're sure you're keeping the item.
- **Set a reminder a few days before the deadline**, not on the day itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### I missed my Amazon return window by one day. Can I still return it?

If you already started the return, yes: drop it off right away. Amazon may charge a late fee of 20% of the item price for the first 30 days after the return-by date. If you never started a return, contact Amazon Customer Service and ask; Amazon doesn't guarantee an exception.

### Can you return something to Amazon after 30 days?

Some items have longer windows, such as 90 days for most nonperishable baby products and mattresses. For most items, a return more than 30 days after delivery isn't guaranteed. If the item is defective, check Amazon Product Support, Your Orders and the manufacturer's warranty instead.

### Does the A-to-z Guarantee cover late returns?

Not for change-of-mind returns. It covers third-party seller orders with a last possible delivery date 90 days ago or less, and only for listed problems, such as a faulty item the seller won't take back or a refund the seller didn't pay.

### Does my credit card have return protection?

Only some cards do. American Express lists specific cards with Return Protection (up to $300 per item), and Chase offers it on the Sapphire Reserve (up to $500 per item). Both require you to file within 90 days of purchase, after the store refuses the return.

### Should I dispute the charge with my card instead?

Think it through first. For third-party seller orders, Amazon says asking your bank or card issuer for a refund or chargeback makes you ineligible for an A-to-z Guarantee refund. Return protection, if your card has it, is a separate benefit from a dispute.

### Which stores give you the most time to return?

Costco lists no general deadline for most products under its satisfaction guarantee, though most electronics have 90 days. Target and Walmart give 90 days for most items, and Target Owned Brand items get a year with a receipt.
