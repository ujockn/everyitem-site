If you can't show that an HSA withdrawal paid for a qualified medical expense, the IRS treats it as taxable income and may add a **20% additional tax** on top, according to [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969). The receipt is what proves the withdrawal was medical. So how long you keep a pharmacy or doctor's receipt depends on what it's backing up: a tax deduction, an HSA withdrawal, or an FSA claim. This guide covers all three, what a usable receipt needs to show, and how to get a copy from CVS or Walgreens if you lost yours. The rules come from the IRS and official pharmacy and benefits pages, linked in [Sources](#sources).

For receipts in general (returns, warranties, insurance), see [how long to keep receipts](/guides/how-long-to-keep-receipts/).

## The short version

| What the receipt backs up | How long to keep it |
| --- | --- |
| Medical expense deduction on Schedule A | At least 3 years after you file that year's return |
| Expense paid with your HSA debit card | At least 3 years after you file the return for the year of the payment |
| Expense you'll reimburse from your HSA later | Until you take the reimbursement, then 3 more years after filing that year's return |
| FSA claim | Until your plan approves the claim, then keep a copy for at least 3 years after filing |
| Glasses, hearing aids or other medical equipment | Also for the full warranty period, and as long as you own it if it's insured |

The 3-year figure is the IRS's general period of limitations for income tax returns. The IRS says it can be longer in some cases, such as **6 years** if you leave out more than 25% of your income, and **indefinitely** if you don't file a return. Those rules are on the IRS page [How long should I keep records?](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)

## Receipts for the medical expense deduction

[IRS Publication 502](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p502) says you can deduct medical and dental expenses on Schedule A only to the extent they're **more than 7.5% of your adjusted gross income**. It says to keep records of those expenses to support the deduction, and not to send them with a paper return.

Two details matter for pharmacy receipts:

- **Prescriptions count, most over-the-counter medicine doesn't.** Publication 502 lets you include prescribed medicines and insulin. Except for insulin, a drug that doesn't need a prescription can't be included, even if your doctor recommended it.
- **No double counting.** You can't deduct expenses you paid with a tax-free HSA distribution, according to Publication 502.

Many medical items you own qualify too. Publication 502 lists eyeglasses and contact lenses needed for medical reasons, hearing aids (plus batteries, repairs and maintenance), crutches and diagnostic devices such as a blood sugar test kit.

## Receipts for HSA withdrawals

With an HSA, you generally don't send receipts anywhere when you pay with the card or move money out. You're expected to keep them yourself. Publication 969 says your records need to show three things:

1. The money was used only to pay for or reimburse **qualified medical expenses**.
2. Those expenses **weren't already paid or reimbursed** from another source, like insurance.
3. You **didn't also deduct** them as an itemized deduction in any year.

The IRS says to keep these records with your tax records and not send them with your return. Your HSA trustee reports every distribution, including debit card purchases, to you and the IRS on Form 1099-SA.

**Why HSA receipts can need to last years.** Publication 969 says you can take tax-free distributions for qualified medical expenses you incur **after you establish the HSA**, and that you don't have to make withdrawals every year. So you can pay a pharmacy bill out of pocket today and pay yourself back from the HSA later. If you do, the receipt has to survive until that later withdrawal, and then for the limitations period of the tax return for that year. In practice, that can mean keeping a receipt for a long time. Expenses from before the HSA was opened don't qualify, so the receipt date matters.

Publication 969 also says the 20% additional tax doesn't apply to distributions made after you become disabled, reach age 65, or die. The withdrawal is still taxable income if it wasn't for medical expenses.

## Receipts for FSA claims

An FSA works the other way around: you usually have to prove the expense **before** you get the money. Publication 969 says you must give the FSA a written statement from an independent third party showing the expense was incurred and how much it was, plus your own statement that no other health coverage paid for it. If an FSA debit card purchase meets certain IRS substantiation methods, Publication 969 says you may not have to provide anything more, but keep the receipt in case your plan follows up.

Timing matters more with an FSA. Publication 969 calls FSAs generally "use-it-or-lose-it": unused money at the end of the plan year is usually forfeited unless your plan has a grace period of up to 2½ months or a limited carryover. Your plan sets its own deadline for sending claims, so check it. FSAFEDS, the FSA program for federal employees, tells members who mail a claim to send copies of their receipts and keep the originals.

Over-the-counter medicine is a covered FSA expense whether or not it's prescribed, according to Publication 969. CVS notes that since the 2020 CARES Act, eligible over-the-counter medicine and products can be bought with HSA or FSA funds without a prescription.

## What a usable medical receipt shows

[FSAFEDS](https://www.fsafeds.gov/file) lists five things a receipt should show for a health care claim, and they make a good checklist for HSA records and tax deductions too:

- **Patient's name** (it can be left off for retail store purchases)
- **Provider or store name**
- **Date** of the service or purchase
- **What it was**, described in detail. FSAFEDS says a pharmacy prescription receipt is enough for prescriptions.
- **Cost**, or the part insurance didn't pay

FSAFEDS also suggests using your insurer's Explanation of Benefits (EOB) when insurance paid part of the bill, and warns that credit card slips, canceled checks and balance-forward statements usually don't include enough detail on their own. Receipts should be readable, not too dark or faded, with nothing cut off.

That last point is the catch with pharmacy receipts: most are printed on thermal paper that fades. Scanning or photographing them the day you get them keeps the text readable for as long as you need it.

## Lost a pharmacy receipt? How to get a copy

You can usually rebuild the record from the pharmacy:

- **CVS:** with Prescription Management added to your CVS.com account, the [Prescription History](https://www.cvs.com/retail/help/help-subtopic-view-print-prescription-history) tab shows fill dates and the amount paid, and you can filter by patient, month and year and print it. CVS also offers a free [report of HSA- and FSA-eligible purchases](https://www.cvs.com/content/fsa) made with your ExtraCare card, in store and online, going back **14 months**. You request it by calling 1-800-SHOP-CVS and pressing 2, and it can arrive by mail or email.
- **Walgreens:** in your Walgreens.com pharmacy account, go to Prescriptions, then Records, choose a date range and print. Walgreens says online prescription history covers the **last 18 months**. For older records, its [Pharmacy Help page](https://www.walgreens.com/topic/help/pharmacyhelp/pharmacy_help_main.jsp) explains how to send a records request form to its Custodian of Records, with a response in up to 15 business days. Walgreens also marks likely FSA-eligible items with an **F** on store receipts.

Both online windows are limited, so don't count on them for an HSA reimbursement you plan to take years from now. Save your own copy.

## Medical things you own: one receipt, three jobs

For prescription glasses, hearing aids, a CPAP machine or a home blood pressure monitor, the receipt does more than back up an HSA withdrawal or a deduction. It's also your proof of purchase for a warranty claim, and your proof of ownership and value if the item is lost in a fire or theft. Keep it for the longest of those: the tax period, the full warranty, and as long as you own the item. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I keep prescription receipts?

At least 3 years after you file the tax return they support, if you deduct them or paid with HSA or FSA money. If you plan to reimburse yourself from an HSA later, keep the receipt until that withdrawal and then for 3 years after filing that year's return.

### How long should I keep HSA receipts?

Long enough to cover the tax return for the year the money came out of your HSA, which is generally 3 years after you file it. The IRS doesn't require you to withdraw HSA money in the same year as the expense, so a receipt for a later reimbursement may need to be kept for years.

### Do I need to send receipts with my tax return?

No. The IRS says to keep HSA records and medical expense records with your tax records instead of sending them with your return.

### Do I need receipts for FSA purchases made with the debit card?

Sometimes. Publication 969 says that if a card purchase meets certain IRS substantiation methods, you may not have to provide more information, but your plan can still ask for proof. Keep the receipt until the claim is settled, and a copy after that.

### Can I deduct over-the-counter medicine?

Generally not on your tax return. IRS Publication 502 allows prescribed medicines and insulin only. Over-the-counter medicine can still be paid for with HSA or FSA money, so keep the receipt for that.

### What if I lost a pharmacy receipt?

Print your prescription history from your pharmacy account. CVS lets you print it from Prescription History and can send a 14-month report of HSA- and FSA-eligible ExtraCare purchases. Walgreens shows 18 months online and takes records requests for older fills.

### Is a credit card statement enough proof of a medical expense?

Usually not on its own. FSAFEDS says credit card slips and canceled checks typically don't show what was bought or who it was for. Use an itemized receipt or an Explanation of Benefits from your insurer.
