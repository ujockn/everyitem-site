Lose a prepaid Visa or Amex gift card, and whatever gets spent before you call the issuer is gone. The [Vanilla Visa Gift Card agreement](https://assets.ctfassets.net/ihysaliiegrh/1Sjbw8V43NKXKY17zFDAr8/fd9801f44b3ee3cd0b6aaecb2a0ccf5b/C1957_403_Dual_Visa_MC_Pathward_VMS_Vanilla_Gift_Physical_Virtual_PR_8_5x11_CHA_v3__2_.pdf) and the [American Express Gift Card agreement](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/prepaid/view-all-cards/gift-cards/terms-and-conditions/) both say amounts debited before you report the loss aren't refunded. The rest of the money is a different story.

The short answer: **the store won't take the card back, but the issuer sometimes will.** The issuer is the bank named on the back of the card, not Visa, Mastercard or the store that sold it. Vanilla's agreement lets you cancel a card you haven't used and ask for a **refund check**. American Express sends the **remaining balance by check** if the card is lost, stolen or past its valid-thru date. Below is what each official page says, linked in [Sources](#sources).

## Visa, Mastercard and Amex gift card refunds: the short answer

| Situation | What happens | Who to contact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return the card to the store | Not accepted: Target and Walmart list these cards as final sale | n/a |
| Unused card you don't want (Vanilla Visa) | Cancel it and request a refund check | Vanilla customer service |
| Unused card you don't want (Amex) | Not redeemable for cash, except where the law requires | American Express |
| Lost or stolen | Remaining balance at the time you report it: a replacement card or a check | The issuer on the back of the card |
| Past the valid-thru date | Funds don't expire: a free replacement (Vanilla) or a refund (Amex) | The issuer |
| Paid a scammer with the card | Report it right away; a refund isn't guaranteed | The issuer, then ReportFraud.ftc.gov |
| Returned something you bought with it | Refund usually goes back onto the card | The store, then check the balance |

## Why the store won't take the card back

The store that sold the card is just the seller. Target's [returns page](https://www.target.com/help/articles/returns-exchanges/returns) says prepaid gift cards, including **Visa, Mastercard and American Express gift cards**, can't be returned or redeemed for cash or credit except where the law requires it. [Walmart's Return Policy](https://corporate.walmart.com/policies) lists **branded gift cards (Visa, MC, Amex)** as final sale in its financial services section.

So a receipt won't get the card taken back at the register. What the receipt *can* do is prove the purchase to the issuer, which is where refunds actually happen. Our [gift card return guide](/guides/return-gift-card/) has the store-by-store rules.

## Who actually decides: the card's issuer

Visa and Mastercard run the payment networks, but they don't hold your balance. Visa's [gift card page](https://www.visa.com/en-us/personal/cards/gift/gift-card-balance) sends you to the card issuer for balances, declines and lost cards, and says the issuer sets the card's limits. [Mastercard's FAQ](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/personal/get-support/frequently-asked-questions.html) does the same: for activation questions, call the customer service number on the back of the card.

That number on the back is the one that matters. Look for a line like "issued by [bank name]" and the cardholder agreement that came in the package. If you threw the packaging away, the agreement is usually on the issuer's website.

## Can you get a refund on a gift card you haven't used?

**It depends on the issuer, so read the agreement first.**

- **Vanilla Visa Gift Card (issued by Pathward):** the [cardholder agreement](https://assets.ctfassets.net/ihysaliiegrh/1Sjbw8V43NKXKY17zFDAr8/fd9801f44b3ee3cd0b6aaecb2a0ccf5b/C1957_403_Dual_Visa_MC_Pathward_VMS_Vanilla_Gift_Physical_Virtual_PR_8_5x11_CHA_v3__2_.pdf) says that if you don't agree to its terms, you shouldn't use the card. Instead, **save your receipt, call customer service, cancel the card and request a refund check.** The same agreement says the card can't be redeemed for its cash value in other cases, and that the activation fee is paid by the purchaser at the time of purchase. It doesn't say whether that fee comes back with the check, so ask when you call.
- **American Express Gift Card:** the [Amex agreement](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/prepaid/view-all-cards/gift-cards/terms-and-conditions/) says the card **isn't redeemable for cash except where required by law**. It also strongly suggests keeping the purchase receipt or email, because Amex may require it for a refund.
- **Any other bank or mall card:** the rules are whatever its agreement says. Call the number on the back and ask directly whether an unused card can be canceled for a refund, and whether any fee is deducted.

Act before you spend anything on the card. Vanilla's refund-check option is written for someone who chooses not to use the card at all.

## Lost or stolen card: what you can get back

All three paths below give back **only what's left when you report it**, so call the same day you notice.

- **Vanilla Visa:** the agreement says to contact customer service immediately with the card number. It **may not be able to help if you wait more than 60 days** after an unauthorized transaction. A replacement card carries the balance from the moment you reported it, and a reissued card can take up to 30 days.
- **American Express:** the agreement and Amex's [lost or stolen card page](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/customer-service/faq.gift-card-lost-stolen.html) ask for the card number, the 3-digit security code on the back and the 4-digit card ID on the front. The agreement says Amex will send the remaining balance **by check or other means**.
- **Cards bought at Target:** Target's [help article](https://www.target.com/help/article/000061972) says Visa, Mastercard or American Express can cancel a lost or stolen card and put the remaining value on a new gift card, with no replacement fee. If you don't have the card number, the **original receipt** should work instead. Target lists 1-877-867-4873 for Visa and Mastercard and 1-833-792-5087 for American Express.

Mastercard's FAQ adds that **registering** a prepaid card is what lets the issuer use your information to replace it. If your card offers registration, do it when you get it.

## Past the valid-thru date? Your money is still there

The date on the front is when the plastic stops working, not when the money disappears.

- Vanilla's agreement says the funds don't expire after the valid-thru date and there's **no fee for a replacement card** sent because the card expired.
- The Amex agreement says the balance stays intact after the valid-thru date and that Amex will **refund the remaining balance by check or other means** instead of issuing a replacement.
- Target says the balances on the Visa, Mastercard and Amex gift cards it sells [never expire](https://www.target.com/help/article/000055610). For a physical card, call the number on the back and they'll send a new one.

Under federal law, the [CFPB explains](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/if-my-prepaid-card-expires-do-i-lose-my-money-en-423/) that gift card funds must stay good for at least five years, and inactivity fees can start only after 12 months without use. Some states give you longer.

## Paid a scammer with a gift card?

The [FDIC says](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2024-12/what-you-should-know-about-gift-cards) one common scam has callers posing as the IRS or a utility company and demanding payment in gift cards. It also warns that, unlike credit cards, there typically isn't any recourse when a gift card is stolen or misused, so the money is hard to get back.

Hard isn't the same as impossible. Do this right away:

1. **Call the issuer** on the back of the card, say the card was used in a scam, and ask whether any balance can be frozen or refunded. The FDIC notes you might get back some or all of what's left, possibly minus a fee.
2. **Have the receipt and card number ready.** The FDIC says you might need both.
3. **Report it** at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) and to your local police.

**Don't count on a chargeback.** The Amex agreement says you can't stop payment or open a billing dispute on purchases made with the card; problems go to the merchant.

## Returning something you bought with the gift card

When you return an item you paid for with one of these cards, the refund normally goes **back onto the same card**, not into your hand as cash:

- Visa says to keep the card even after it hits $0, because you'll **need the actual card to make a return**, and the merchant may put the money back on it.
- Mastercard says the value of returned merchandise is credited back to the card.
- Target's returns page says refunds for purchases made with a third-party gift card are **reloaded onto that gift card**.
- The Amex agreement says a merchant's credit can take **up to seven days** to show in the balance. Vanilla's says the credit may not be available for a number of days after the refund.

So don't toss an "empty" gift card until the item's return window has closed. ## What to keep so a refund is possible

Almost every path above starts with the same question: can you prove the card is yours?

- **The purchase receipt** (or the email for an eGift card). Amex says it may require one for a refund. Target accepts it in place of a lost card number.
- **The card number and security codes**, written down or photographed and stored somewhere other than your wallet. Amex says you'll need them to check a balance or get a refund if the card is lost.
- **The cardholder agreement**, or at least the issuer's name and phone number.
- **Receipts for anything you bought with the card,** until each return window closes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I return a Visa gift card?

Not to the store. Target and Walmart treat Visa, Mastercard and Amex gift cards as final sale, even with the receipt. Your options are with the issuer on the back of the card. Vanilla, for example, lets you cancel a card you haven't used and request a refund check.

### Can you get a refund on a Mastercard gift card?

Sometimes, from the issuer, not from Mastercard or the store. Call the number on the back and ask whether an unused card can be canceled for a refund. For a lost or stolen card, the issuer may replace the card or refund whatever balance remained when you reported it. Mastercard says registering the card is what makes replacement possible.

### Can Visa gift cards be refunded for cash?

Generally not. The Vanilla Visa agreement says the card can't be redeemed for its cash value, and the store won't refund it. The exceptions are a refund check for an unused card you cancel, and cases where the law requires a cash payout.

### Can I get a refund on an American Express gift card?

The Amex agreement says the card isn't redeemable for cash except where the law requires it. If it's lost, stolen or past its valid-thru date, Amex sends the remaining balance by check or other means. Call 1-877-297-4438 with the card number and both security codes.

### What happens to the money on an expired Visa gift card?

It's still yours. The expiration date applies to the plastic, not the funds. Vanilla sends a free replacement card, and Amex refunds the balance. Under federal law, gift card funds must last at least five years.

### What if someone used my gift card before I reported it lost?

That part usually isn't refunded. Vanilla and Amex both say amounts debited before you report the loss aren't refunded, and Vanilla may not be able to help after 60 days. Report it as soon as you notice.

### Can I dispute a charge on a prepaid gift card?

Not like a credit card. The Amex agreement says you can't stop payment or open a billing dispute on gift card purchases. Take the problem to the merchant. If a scam was involved, call the issuer right away and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

### Where does my refund go if I return something I bought with a Visa gift card?

Usually back onto the same gift card. Visa says to keep the card for returns, Mastercard says returned merchandise is credited to the card, and Target reloads third-party gift card refunds onto the card. It can take several days to show.
