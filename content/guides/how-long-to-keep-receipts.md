This guide explains how long to keep receipts in plain English, sorted by why you're keeping them: returns, warranties, insurance or taxes. The tax rules come from the IRS, linked in [Sources](#sources). This isn't tax advice. If your situation is complicated, ask a tax professional.

**Last checked: Nov 1, 2026.** Every Item is an independent guide and is not affiliated with the IRS or any store.

## The short version

| Why you're keeping it | How long |
| --- | --- |
| Returns or exchanges | Until the store's return window closes (often 14–90 days) |
| Warranty claims | For the whole warranty period, including any extended warranty or protection plan |
| Home or renters insurance | As long as you own the item |
| Tax deductions or credits | Generally 3 years after you file the return, sometimes longer (see below) |
| Property you'll sell or depreciate | Until the limitations period ends for the year you sell or dispose of it |
| Everyday purchases you've kept and used | Once the return window closes and there's no warranty, you can usually let it go |

## Receipts for returns

Most stores want a receipt or order number for a full refund to your original payment method. Without one, you may get store credit or nothing. Return windows vary a lot by store and by item. See our guides to the [Walmart](/guides/walmart-return-policy/), [Target](/guides/target-return-policy/), [Amazon](/guides/amazon-return-policy/), [Costco](/guides/costco-return-policy/) and [Best Buy](/guides/best-buy-return-policy/) return policies. If a deadline has already passed, read [what to do if you missed a return window](/guides/missed-return-window/).

Keep the receipt **until the return window closes**, including any extended holiday window.

## Receipts for warranties

Manufacturers usually ask for proof of purchase showing the date and seller. Keep the receipt for the **full warranty period**. Keep it longer if you bought a protection plan, since those often run past the manufacturer's warranty. Store it with the model and serial number so a claim takes minutes, not an afternoon.

## Receipts for home and renters insurance

If something is stolen or destroyed, your insurer may ask you to show you owned it and what it was worth. Receipts, photos and serial numbers all help. Keep receipts for valuable items **for as long as you own them**. See [how to make a home inventory for insurance](/guides/home-inventory-for-insurance/).

## Receipts for taxes: what the IRS says

The IRS says to keep records that support income, deductions or credits on your tax return **until the period of limitations for that return runs out**:

- **3 years** in most cases.
- **3 years from filing or 2 years from paying the tax, whichever is later,** if you file a claim for a credit or refund.
- **6 years** if you leave out income worth more than 25% of the gross income on your return.
- **7 years** if you claim a loss from worthless securities or a bad debt deduction.
- **Indefinitely** if you don't file a return or you file a fraudulent one.
- **At least 4 years** for employment tax records.

For property, the IRS says to keep records **until the limitations period ends for the year you dispose of the property**. It also notes that insurers or creditors may want records kept longer than the IRS requires.

## Digitize your paper receipts

Most store receipts are printed on thermal paper, which fades with heat, light and time. A receipt in a hot car, a wallet or a kitchen drawer can be blank by the time you need it. Paper also gets lost, crumpled or thrown out by accident. A digital copy fixes all of that.

**How to digitize a receipt well:**

- **Do it the same day.** Scan or photograph the receipt before it goes in a bag or drawer, while the ink is still sharp.
- **Get the whole receipt.** Lay it flat on a dark surface in good light, and make sure the store name, date, items, total and payment method are all readable.
- **Save it with the item.** Attach the copy to the thing you bought, along with its return deadline and warranty end date, so you can find it in seconds.
- **Back it up.** Keep your copies somewhere that survives a lost phone, such as a cloud backup or an exported file.
- **Keep the original for big purchases.** For expensive items, warranties that ask for the original, or anything tied to taxes, keep the paper too, stored flat somewhere cool and dark.

Once a receipt is digitized, you can toss the paper for everyday purchases after the return window closes.

## How to never lose a receipt again

- Digitize the receipt the day you buy the item.
- Note the return deadline and the warranty end date with it.
- Group items by room, so an insurance list already exists if you need one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I keep receipts for taxes?

Generally 3 years after you file the return they support. The IRS says to keep them 6 years if you left out more than 25% of your gross income, 7 years for worthless securities or bad debt losses, and indefinitely if you didn't file.

### Do I need to keep grocery receipts?

Usually not, once you're sure you won't return anything. Keep them only if they support a tax deduction or a reimbursement.

### Is a photo of a receipt good enough?

For your own records and many store or warranty claims, a clear copy is often accepted, but each store or manufacturer decides. Keep the original for expensive items if you can.

### How long should I keep receipts for appliances and electronics?

At least for the warranty period, and for as long as you own them if you want proof for an insurance claim.
